The Challenge

Kaiser Permanente's Video Visit experience connected patients and clinicians across web, iOS, and Android. The product had to support two very different users while maintaining a coherent experience across a complex healthcare ecosystem.

The challenge was not simply improving individual screens. It was creating a scalable product experience across multiple platforms while balancing clinical workflows, accessibility requirements, technical constraints, operational needs, and multiple teams contributing to the same product.

My Role

As Lead Product Designer, I worked across product strategy, UX direction, interaction design, accessibility, design systems, and cross-functional alignment.

My responsibility extended beyond delivering UI. I helped establish experience direction, identify systemic problems, translate complex clinical requirements into usable workflows, and create patterns that teams could implement consistently across the ecosystem.

My Contribution

  • Led UX strategy and experience direction for complex telemedicine workflows.
  • Translated patient, clinical, business, and technical requirements into product decisions.
  • Defined mobile user flows for patient entry, video visit access, language selection, permissions, connection states, and critical interaction states.
  • Created interaction models, wireframes, prototypes, and high-fidelity UI designs to validate mobile workflows before development.
  • Established reusable interaction and UI patterns to improve cross-platform consistency.
  • Embedded ADA, Section 508, and WCAG requirements into the design process.
  • Created reusable accessibility and compliance guidance for distributed product teams.
  • Partnered with Product, Engineering, Clinical, Architecture, QA, and distributed Design teams.
  • Participated in roadmap refinement and SAFe Program Increment planning.

Outcome

The work contributed to a more consistent and accessible telemedicine experience across Kaiser Permanente's patient and clinician ecosystem.

Reusable design patterns and accessibility guidance helped distributed teams solve recurring experience problems more consistently, while improving alignment between Design, Product, Engineering, and clinical stakeholders.

The work was also recognized with Stevie® Award recognition for enhancing the patient and clinician experience.

Product Thinking

Design Around Workflows, Not Screens

Rather than treating individual screens as isolated UX problems, I mapped the broader telemedicine ecosystem across the patient and clinician journey.

Patient entry, appointment context, video visit, clinician workflow, scheduling, status, and follow-up all had to work as one connected experience.

This shifted the design conversation from screen-level optimization to system-level product thinking.

Accessibility

Accessibility as Product Architecture

Accessibility was treated as a product requirement from the beginning, not as a final QA checkpoint.

I applied ADA, Section 508, and WCAG requirements throughout the design process and helped establish reusable guidance for distributed teams.

The objective was to make accessible design repeatable across teams rather than requiring each designer to solve the same problems independently.

Designing for High-Density Clinical Work

Clinicians operate under very different constraints than patients. Their experience requires fast access to relevant information, clear status, predictable interaction patterns, and minimal cognitive overhead.

I focused on information hierarchy, workflow dependencies, interaction states, and operational context rather than simply reducing visual complexity.

The result was a more deliberate approach to designing high-density clinical workflows without compromising usability or accessibility.

Designing the Mobile Patient Journey

The mobile experience had to make a complex clinical interaction feel simple for patients, often when they were joining a visit from an unfamiliar environment or device.

I designed the mobile workflow around the critical moments in the journey: entering the visit, selecting a language, granting device permissions, understanding connection status, and recovering from errors or interruptions.

Rather than simply translating the web experience to mobile, I adapted the interaction model to screen size, touch behavior, and platform conventions across iOS and Android.

I used user flows, interaction models, wireframes, prototypes, and high-fidelity UI designs to work through the experience with Product and Engineering before implementation.

The goal was a predictable and accessible mobile experience that kept patients focused on the primary task: successfully connecting with their care team.

Design System

Creating Consistency Across the Ecosystem

Multiple teams contributed to different parts of the product, creating a natural risk of inconsistent patterns and interaction models.

I helped establish reusable UI and interaction patterns that could scale across web, iOS, Android, and tablet experiences.

The goal was not to make every platform identical. It was to establish what should remain consistent and where platform or workflow-specific differences were appropriate.

Cross-Functional Leadership

Aligning Design, Product, Engineering & Clinical Teams

Complex healthcare products rarely have a single decision-maker. Clinical requirements, product priorities, engineering constraints, accessibility standards, and operational needs all influence the final experience.

I worked across Product, Engineering, Clinical, Architecture, QA, and distributed Design teams to surface constraints early and create alignment around experience decisions.

Working within SAFe Program Increment planning also required translating design priorities into a delivery environment with dependencies, sequencing, and technical constraints.

Leadership Insight

The Designer's Job Is to Improve the Decision

The most valuable contribution was not any individual interface. It was creating design alignment across a complex product ecosystem.

In healthcare, designers rarely control all of the variables. Clinical requirements, accessibility, technology, operations, and business priorities all shape the final experience.

My role was to make those constraints visible, establish the right product principles, and help teams make better decisions despite them.

That became the core of my Lead Product Designer role on the Video Visit experience.
